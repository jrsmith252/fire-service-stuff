RTC: Car into Building

ICL1 Development Scenario

This scenario places you in command of an RTC where a vehicle has collided with a commercial building. You will work through the incident from mobilisation to closure, making decisions and considering your actions at each phase.

🎯 Learning Objectives

On completion of this scenario, you will have practised:

  • Conducting a structured scene assessment using the 6 phases of RTC
  • Developing a casualty-centred rescue plan with A-plan, B-plan and immediate extrication plan options
  • Identifying and managing structural stability hazards at RTC incidents
  • Briefing incoming senior officers using the OTHERS framework
📖 NFRS Learn: RTC Modules

Estimated time: 20-30 minutes

PHASE 1: TURNOUT / MOBILISATION

You are the Officer in Charge of T05P1 Ashfield when you receive the following mobilisation:

xx/xx/xx 05:00:00 Alert messaging for station: ASHFIELD

12 LEAMINGTON DRIVE
SUTTON IN ASHFIELD
NG17 5AY

YOUR APPLIANCE: T05P1 (F_WRLW)
ALSO MOBILISED: T05P2 - Ashfield, T16R1, T16P1, TS01

💭 Consider the following:

  • What are your initial considerations based on this information?
  • What does "car into building" tell you about potential hazards?
  • What significance does the time of call (05:00) have?
  • What resources are responding and what capabilities do they bring?

Key points an IC should consider:

  • P1 indicates persons trapped - lifesaving is the priority
  • Structural stability concerns - vehicle has impacted building, potential for collapse
  • 05:00 hours - darkness, limited visibility, commercial premises likely closed
  • Consider if there are flats above commercial premises - potential occupants
  • T16R1 bringing heavy rescue capability; TS01 providing tactical command (may not be in attendance immediately)
  • Brief crew on expected 6 phases of RTC approach
  • EMAS will be attending as part of the PDA. Consider HART

Visual Cues Analysis

Examine the scene carefully. What visual cues can you identify and what do they tell you?

Cue Category What Is Present Considerations
Vehicle Position White hatchback (VW Golf type) embedded in building frontage, lights and indicator illuminated (electrics active, engine may still be running) Consider benefit(s) of isolating vehicle electrics vs risk of crew exposure to potential collapse zone
Structural Damage Visible cracking in brickwork above impact point extending to first floor level - structural stability compromised Consider benefits vs risk of structural shoring. Seek advice from specialist / TacAd. Establish exclusion zone

Exposures Adjacent "Clothes Shop" premises, residential properties opposite with lights on Witnesses may have information. Consider cordon to manage public. Adjacent premises may need evacuation if collapse risk extends

💭 Consider the following:

  • What are your immediate actions on arrival?
  • What are the main hazards and how will you control them?
  • Where would you position yourself and why?
  • How will you deploy your resources?
  • What is your Incident Action Plan?

Apply the 6 Phases of RTC:

  1. Scene Assessment - inner/outer survey, information gathering
  2. Scene Safety - cordons, hazard control, safe approach
  3. Glass Management - consider leaving in place for structural support
  4. Space Creation - method to access casualty; consider relocation of vehicle using SRU winch, seek specialist advice
  5. Full Access - create egress route for casualty
  6. Immobilisation and Extrication - coordinate with ambulance

Key points:

  • Establish collapse/exclusion zone around damaged structure
  • Triage casualties - pedestrian on ground may be higher priority than trapped driver
  • Request lighting - critical for night-time scene
  • Consider if anyone in the flat above - door-knock to check

📋 Command Decision Crosshairs

🎯 Objectives

  • Save saveable life (driver, pedestrian casualty)
  • Prevent escalation (structural collapse, fire)
  • Protect the environment (fuel spillage)

⚡ Priorities

  • Effect rescue of trapped driver
  • First aid to pedestrian casualty
  • Establish exclusion zone around collapse risk
  • Isolate vehicle electrics/fuel
  • Safety officer to monitor structure

⚠️ Hazards

  • Structural collapse - cracking visible
  • Vehicle fuel system
  • Un-deployed airbags
  • Live electrics in vehicle
  • Glass/debris
  • Working at night - visibility

👥 People / To Do

  • 1 driver trapped (back pain, spinal precautions)
  • 1 pedestrian casualty on pavement
  • Flat above confirmed unoccupied
  • EMAS on scene - coordinate extrication
  • SM arriving - prepare OTHERS brief

Tactical priorities:

  • Establish safe working environment - exclusion zone around collapse risk
  • Structured casualty assessment in absence of paramedic
  • Back pain = spinal precautions, controlled extrication, coordinate with ambulance
  • Alcohol smell - note for handover to police, does not change rescue approach
  • The following are suggestions - your plans may differ based on your assessment:
  • A-plan (example): Tunnelling - space creation and extrication from rear of vehicle
  • B-plan (example): Half-roof removal to create alternative egress
  • Immediate/snatch rescue (example): Winch car out from building using SRU, remove driver door for rapid access

📝 OTHERS Briefing Framework

Structure your handover using OTHERS:

O Objectives - operational objectives achieved and outstanding
T Tactical Mode - current tactical mode
H Hazards - identified hazards and current risk assessment
E Existing deployments - existing and proposed deployments
R Resources - resources on site and those requested
S Structure - structure of communications and messages sent
O Objectives: Save life - driver extrication in progress, pedestrian casualty receiving first aid. Outstanding - complete extrication, scene safety.
T Tactical Mode: Offensive
H Hazards: Structural instability - cracking visible to first floor, exclusion zone established. Vehicle fuel/electrics isolated. Un-deployed airbags.
E Existing deployments: Crew from T05P1 on extrication. T05P2 crew managing pedestrian casualty. SRU crew standing by for winching and shoring. Safety officer monitoring structure.
R Resources: Two pumps and an SRU in attendance.
S Structure: Command support is currently being established.
PHASE 6: INCIDENT CLOSURE

The casualty has been successfully extricated and handed over to the ambulance service. You now need to consider incident closure.

💭 Consider the following:

  • What handover arrangements are required now the casualty is extricated?
  • What agencies need to be involved post-rescue?
  • What scene preservation considerations are there?
  • What welfare considerations are there for your crews?
  • Structured handover to ambulance - SAMPLE or similar
  • Police scene - suspected drink driving, potential for criminal investigation
  • Building control/structural engineer - dangerous structure, may need to be made safe before handover
  • Scene preservation - do not move vehicle until police confirm
  • Utilities - check gas/electric supply to premises, isolate if required
  • Premises owner/keyholder notification via police
  • Crew welfare - hot debrief, any exposure to distressing scenes
